Beginner crop guide

Plan before you plant.

Use this simple guide to think through soil, season, crop choice, planting, water use and crop protection. Local agricultural advice should always guide final decisions.

Start the guideView training pathway
01

Know your soil

Healthy crops begin below the surface.

Observe

Check texture, drainage, colour, erosion and whether water remains after rainfall.

Improve

Add well-prepared organic matter and avoid burning residues or overworking wet soil.

Test

Where possible, use a basic soil test before applying fertilizer or lime.

Practical check: Take a small handful of moist soil. If it forms a smooth sticky ribbon, it may contain more clay. If it falls apart quickly, it may be sandy.
02

Choose the right crop

Match the crop to your conditions and goals.

ConsiderAsk yourself
SeasonIs rainfall expected, or will irrigation be available?
SoilDoes the crop suit the soil type and drainage?
TimeHow long will the crop take to mature?
PurposeIs it for household food, livestock, processing or sale?
MarketWho may buy it, and what quality do they expect?
RiskCan you start on a small plot before expanding?
03

Plant carefully

Good timing and spacing reduce avoidable losses.

1

Prepare the land

Clear responsibly, control erosion and keep useful organic material where appropriate.

2

Use healthy seed or planting material

Select reliable varieties suited to local conditions and avoid visibly damaged material.

3

Follow recommended spacing

Overcrowding increases competition for light, water and nutrients.

4

Record the planting date

A simple notebook helps track growth, inputs, rainfall and harvest timing.

04

Manage water

Use water efficiently and protect the soil.

Water at the right time

Early morning or late afternoon can reduce evaporation.

Keep moisture in the soil

Mulching may reduce water loss and weed pressure when used correctly.

Prevent standing water

Drainage is important for crops that do not tolerate waterlogged roots.

05

Protect crops responsibly

Observe early and act carefully.

1

Inspect regularly

Look under leaves, around stems and across the field for unusual changes.

2

Identify the problem

Do not assume every symptom requires pesticide. Water stress and nutrient problems can look similar to disease.

3

Use integrated methods

Combine sanitation, resistant varieties, crop rotation, physical control and expert advice.

4

Handle chemicals safely

Follow the approved label, use protective equipment and keep products away from children, food and water sources.

Safety: AgriSmart educational content does not replace advice from qualified local agricultural officers, agronomists or product labels.
