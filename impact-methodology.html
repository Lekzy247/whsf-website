How WHSF measures impact.

This methodology explains the meaning, source and limitations of public figures. It is a living framework and will be updated as WHSF strengthens programme-level monitoring and independent verification.

Measurement framework

From participation to sustained opportunity.

WHSF aims to report results at seven levels. Not every historic programme has complete data at every level; public reports should state gaps clearly.

Reach

Unique participants enrolled or attending a recorded activity, with duplicate records removed where identifiers allow.

Completion

Participants meeting the published attendance, assignment or assessment requirement for a programme.

Learning

Change measured through pre/post assessments, demonstrated skills, completed projects or instructor rubrics.

Inclusion

Participation reported by age, gender, disability, location and rural or underserved context where safe and consented.

Opportunity

Verified internships, jobs, scholarships, further education, mentorship or enterprise pathways following participation.

Community & sustainability

Projects delivered, people indirectly reached, trainers retained, schools returning and partners continuing support.

Data principles

  • Collect only what is necessary and proportionate.
  • Use consent and safeguarding-aware processes.
  • Separate unique participants from attendances.
  • State the reporting period and geographic scope.
  • Correct public figures when better evidence becomes available.

Next reporting priorities

  • Programme-level enrolment and completion rates.
  • Pre/post learning assessments.
  • Country, community and inclusion breakdowns.
  • Six- and twelve-month education or employment follow-up.
  • Annual impact report with data notes.
