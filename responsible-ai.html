Responsible AI principles

AI should expand human agency—not replace it.

These principles guide WHSF’s AI learning, demonstrations and developing tools. They are an operational commitment, not a claim that every planned system is already deployed or independently audited.

Human oversight

People remain responsible for consequential decisions. AI output should be reviewed before it affects education, employment, safety, immigration, health or access to opportunity.

Clear limitations

Every AI feature should state whether it is available, a pilot, in development or a concept demonstration—and explain what users must verify elsewhere.

Privacy and necessity

Collect the minimum data needed, explain its purpose, restrict access and avoid using sensitive participant information to train models without specific authority and consent.

Fairness and inclusion

Design and test with underserved communities, women, young people, persons with disabilities and different languages, locations and levels of connectivity in mind.

Safety and safeguarding

Apply additional controls where children or vulnerable participants may use AI. Provide reporting, escalation and human-support pathways.

Evidence and accountability

Document intended use, data sources, known risks, responsible owners, evaluation results, incidents and material changes.

High-risk boundaries

What WHSF tools must not imply.

  • AI career matches are recommendations, not guarantees of employment or suitability.
  • Immigration information is not legal advice and must link to current official authorities.
  • Health information is educational and must not diagnose or replace a qualified professional.
  • Automated systems must not make final safeguarding or eligibility decisions without authorised human review.
  • Credentials must not be described as verified unless the issuing and verification process is operational.

Before a pilot

  • Name an accountable programme owner.
  • Complete a proportionate risk and privacy review.
  • Define intended users and excluded uses.
  • Test accessibility and bias risks.
  • Prepare user notices, support and incident response.

Questions or concerns

Report an AI safety, privacy, accessibility or safeguarding concern through the WHSF contact form.
